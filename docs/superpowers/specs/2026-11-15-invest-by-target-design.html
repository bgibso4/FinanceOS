Invest by target

When you have money to invest, you open your Sheets tracker. "Asset Allocation" sets your split (stocks, fixed income, crypto). "Global Index Allocation" splits the stock part across regions by market size, and you type in an amount, each fund's value and its price to see what to buy. FinanceOS already knows your holdings and prices, so this moves that tool into the app. You set your targets once on a new Investing Targets settings tab. Then, on the Investments page, you type an amount and get a list of funds to buy that moves your whole portfolio toward those targets. It only suggests buys unless you turn on sells. It also fixes the sheet's calculation bugs. Per-purchase tracking (issue #30) stays a separate, later piece of work.

Waiting on you

My own calls. Each is easy to change now; veto any you don't like.

  • M1Classes and regions are short lists you can edit
    The classes start as Stocks 80, Fixed income 15 and Crypto 5, as in your sheet's Asset Allocation tab. Stocks is split into US, Developed ex-US and Emerging. You can rename, add or remove either kind. Only one class (Stocks) is split by region. Each country belongs to exactly one region.
    Targets
  • M2Each fund counts toward one target
    A fund counts toward a region of Stocks, toward another class, or not at all. Splitting one fund across several targets (an all-world fund like VT) is left out, because none of your funds need it.
    Counted
  • M3Anything not counted is left out, and shown
    Single stocks (such as the employer stock), target-date funds, cash and money market funds don't count toward a target. The drawer lists what was left out and its total, so nothing disappears silently. Uninvested cash is left out because it's what you're about to invest.
    Counted
  • M4The tilt is a percentage on any region
    Each region has a tilt that starts at 0. Setting the US to +20% means the US gets 1.2 × its market weight within Stocks, and the regions are then scaled back to 100% of Stocks. For example, US 56.3% of stocks becomes about 60.7%.
    Targets
  • M5Buys go where the gaps are, in proportion
    With sells off, the deposit goes only to targets you're below, in proportion to how far below each one is. If the deposit is more than all the gaps together, the gaps are filled first and the rest is split by target. Nothing goes to a target you're already over.
    Math
  • M6Totals that don't add to 100% are scaled, with a note
    Your country weights add to about 99.3%. The tool scales them, and class percentages likewise, to 100%, and shows a note such as "Your country weights add to 99.34%; scaled to 100%" so you can fix the numbers if you want.
    Targets
  • M7Starting data comes from your sheet
    The first setup fills in the classes (80 / 15 / 5) and the countries and weights from the region table on "Global Index Allocation 2026", with Canada (3.55%) moved to Developed ex-US. The "buy with" funds start empty for you to pick. Country weights are public data, but account names stay out of the public repo, so you leave out the 401(k) and DSP yourself on the tab rather than through a seed.
    Data
  • M8Two funds for one slice means two classes
    To hold both gold and bonds, make two classes, such as "Gold 7.5" and "Bonds 7.5", each with its own "buy with" fund. Giving classes their own split, as Stocks has, can come later if this gets clunky.
    Targets
  • M9It doesn't record anything
    The drawer is a calculator. It doesn't save the plan or the orders you place; your next sync brings in the buys. The sheet's "previous market value" column goes, since the app already shows change over time.
    Scope
  • M10Sells are shown in dollars, with the funds they'd come from
    With "Allow sells" on, a target you're over shows a sell amount in dollars, not shares. What you hold there may not be the "buy with" fund (VIU in Questrade, say), so a share count could be wrong. Under the row it lists what you hold there, by account, so you choose what to sell.
    Math
  • M11Accounts with only a typed-in balance are left out
    An account where you type in a balance by hand has no funds to assign to targets. It's listed under "Left out" as a whole account, even when ticked. To count it, enter its holdings instead (the holdings editor already supports this).
    Counted
  • M12A "buy with" fund doesn't have to be held yet
    The picker offers any market-priced security, and you can add one by ticker as you do today. Daily price updates also cover every "buy with" fund, so one you haven't bought yet still has a current price.
    Data

Scope

Goals

  • Type an amount, get a list of funds to buy, in dollars and shares
  • Measured across counted accounts, with live values and prices
  • Class and region targets you update yearly, with an optional US tilt
  • The sheet's calculation bugs gone

Non-goals

  • Per-purchase lots and tax lots (#30)
  • Fetching country weights automatically (later)
  • Placing orders, or recording a plan
  • Splitting one fund across targets, or a class across funds (M2, M8)
  • Choosing classes per deposit

Anti-goals

  • Suggesting a sell when sells are off
  • A buy list that hides what it left out
  • Numbers that disagree with the Investments page totals
  • Account names or balances committed to the public repo

Approaches

For how the stock targets are set. Asset classes were added on top afterwards, once we read the sheet's Asset Allocation tab.

1 · Targets

  • Asset classes: each has a name, a target % and, unless it's split by region, a "buy with" fund. They start as Stocks 80 (split by region), Fixed income 15 and Crypto 5. Class percentages are scaled to 100% if they don't add up (M6).
  • Countries: each has a name, a weight (%) and a region. They start with the numbers from the sheet's region table (M7):
    • US: USA 55.9
    • Developed ex-US: Japan 7.4, UK 4.1, France 2.9, Switzerland 2.6, Germany 2.6, Australia 2.1, Other developed 2.77, Canada 3.55
    • Emerging: China 5.4, Taiwan 1.7, Other emerging 8.32 (South Korea is inside it, as in the sheet)
    They add up to 99.34%, so the scaling note shows. The sheet's "Global Market Distribution" list says 2.4 for Canada, but the region table used 3.55; this keeps 3.55, which is what your past plans were based on.
  • A region's share of stocks = the sum of its countries' weights × (1 + tilt), with all regions then scaled to 100% (M4).
  • The final targets are a single flat list: each region (its share × the Stocks class %), then every other class at its own %. The calculation runs on this list. With the starting data and no tilt: US 45.0%, Developed ex-US 22.6%, Emerging 12.4%, Fixed income 15%, Crypto 5%.
  • A target with no "buy with" fund shows "Pick a fund to buy" instead of a share count; its dollars are still shown. A region with no countries, or a class at 0, has a 0% target.

2 · What gets counted

  • Holdings come from CurrentHoldingsReader, the same source as the Investments page, valued in base currency with the existing RateBook, so the two pages agree.
  • An account counts unless it's unticked on Investing Targets. Only investment accounts (trackingMode: 'investment') are listed.
  • A holding counts toward a target when its security is assigned to one (M2). A fund held in several accounts adds up across them. All other holdings go into the "Left out" list with their values (M3).
  • An account with only a typed-in balance (the reader's per-account placeholder holding) is left out as a whole account and listed under "Left out" (M11). It doesn't appear on the Funds card.
  • Each target's current value = the sum of its counted holdings. The counted total is the sum across targets.

3 · The calculation

This is a pure function. It takes each final target's current value and target %, the amount, and whether sells are on, and returns dollars per target. Turning dollars into shares is a separate step.

  • After-deposit total T = counted total + amount. Gap per target g = target% × T − current. The gaps always add up to the amount.
  • Sells on: each target's trade is its gap; a negative trade is a sell. Afterwards every target is exactly on. Buys get shares as below. Sells show dollars only, with what you hold there listed by account (M10).
  • Sells off (default): let G = the sum of the positive gaps.
    • If amount ≥ G: every target below gets its full gap, and the remaining amount − G is split by target %.
    • Otherwise each target below gets amount × g / G, and targets you're over get 0.
    • No trade is ever negative, and the buys add up to the amount to the cent. Any rounding remainder goes on the largest buy.
  • Shares: dollars are converted to the fund's currency with RateBook and divided by its latest stored price. Shares show to 3 decimals, since Fidelity buys fractional shares. The price's date is shown, and flagged if it's more than 3 days old.
  • Each row also shows the target's current %, its % after the trade, and its target %.
Worked example (sells off)Counted total $8,000, with three targets: US $4,700, Developed $2,000, Emerging $1,300. Targets are 60 / 25 / 15 and the amount is $1,000, so T = $9,000. The gaps are: US 5,400 − 4,700 = +700, Developed 2,250 − 2,000 = +250, Emerging 1,350 − 1,300 = +50. G = 1,000, which equals the amount, so the buys are $700 / $250 / $50 and every target lands exactly on.

4 · Screens

As in the approved mock.

  • Investments page: an Invest button in the header opens a right-side drawer that pushes the page aside.
  • Drawer:
    • Amount card: an amount field in USD, quick amounts ($500, $1,000, $2,500, $5,000), and an "Allow sells" switch that starts off and explains what it does.
    • "Buy list" card ("Rebalance" with sells on, "Where you stand" with no amount): one row per final target. Each row shows the fund ticker, its name, and a target tag such as "Stocks · US". Next to that: now → after % with a small bar (target as a tick), the target %, and the buy in dollars with shares at the price. The card ends with a total line.
    • Notes: totals scaled (M6), the price date and CAD rate, and "Targets already over get nothing" when that applies.
    • "Left out": collapsed, showing its total and number of items; it expands to show each item and why it's left out.
    • Footer: "Nothing is saved", and "Edit investing targets →".
    The drawer recalculates as you type.
  • Settings → Investing Targets (after Securities), with five cards:
    • Asset classes: name, target % and "buy with" fund (Stocks says "Split by region, below"), plus the total.
    • Stocks by region: name, market weight, tilt, share of stocks, overall target and "buy with" fund.
    • Countries: name, weight and region, with the total.
    • Funds: each held fund, which accounts hold it, its value, and the target it counts toward (or Not counted).
    • Accounts: a tick box per investment account.
  • Empty state: before any targets exist, the drawer says "Set your investing targets first" and links to the tab.

5 · Data

  • New TargetClass (id, name, pct Float, sortOrder, splitByRegion Boolean @default(false), buySecurityId String? → Security, SetNull). At most one class may split by region; the API enforces it.
  • New TargetRegion (id, name, sortOrder, tiltPct Float @default(0), buySecurityId String? → Security, SetNull). Regions belong to whichever class splits by region.
  • New TargetCountry (id, name, weightPct Float, regionId → TargetRegion, Cascade).
  • Security.targetClassId String? and Security.targetRegionId String?, both SetNull. A security assigned to the split class must have a region; one assigned to any other class has none. Neither set means Not counted.
  • Account.inTargets Boolean @default(true).
  • Apply with prisma db push, not migrate dev.
  • The cloud-sync export and import carry the new tables and fields. The tables go after Security, which they reference.
  • The price refresh (src/lib/prices/refresh.ts) also covers every class and region buySecurityId (M12).
  • The default classes, regions and countries (M7) are created the first time the tab or the drawer loads with no classes, not by a migration. They're public numbers, so they can live in code.
  • Routes go under /api/investments/targets: one to read everything, and updates for classes, regions, countries, fund assignments and account inclusion. /api/investments/targets/plan?amount=&sells= returns rows, left-out items and notes, and the drawer renders that. Zod validation as usual.
  • A TargetPlanner service is built in services.ts, with holdings, rates and prices injected. It works out the final targets (section 1) and calls the pure calculation (section 3), and both are unit-tested on their own.
