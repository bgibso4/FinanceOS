Invest by target

When you have money to invest, you open your Sheets tracker's "Global Index Allocation" tab, type in the amount, each fund's current value and its price, and it tells you what to buy. FinanceOS already knows your holdings and prices, so this moves that tool into the app. You type an amount and get a buy list that moves your whole portfolio toward a market-size target across a few regions, with an optional US tilt. It only suggests buys unless you turn on sells. It also fixes the sheet's calculation bugs. Per-purchase tracking (issue #30) stays a separate, later piece of work.

Waiting on you

My own calls. Each is easy to change now; veto any you don't like. M2 takes back something I offered in chat.

  • M1Regions are a short list you can edit
    It starts with three: US, Developed ex-US, and Emerging. You can rename them, add one, or remove one. Each country in the weight list belongs to exactly one region.
    Targets
  • M2Each fund counts toward one region
    In chat I offered to split an all-world fund like VT across regions. None of the funds you buy need that, so I've left it out. A fund belongs to one region or to none. It can be added later if you ever buy a multi-region fund.
    Counted
  • M3Anything without a region is left out, and shown
    Single stocks, crypto, cash, money market and target-date funds don't count toward the regions. The drawer lists what was left out and its total, so nothing disappears silently. Uninvested cash is left out too, since it's what you're about to invest.
    Counted
  • M4The tilt is a percentage on any region
    Each region has a tilt that starts at 0. Setting the US to +20% means the US gets 1.2 × its market weight, and every region is then scaled so the total is 100%. For example, US 56.3% becomes about 60.7%. Only the US would use it today, but putting it on each region costs nothing extra.
    Targets
  • M5Buys go where the gaps are, in proportion
    With sells off, the deposit goes only to regions below target, in proportion to how far below they are. If the deposit is more than all the gaps together, the gaps are filled first and the rest is split by target. Nothing goes to a region that's already over target.
    Math
  • M6Weights that don't add to 100% are scaled, with a note
    Your list adds to about 99.2%. The tool scales it to 100% and shows a note like "Your weights add to 99.24%; scaled to 100%" so you can fix it if you want.
    Targets
  • M7Targets live in a new Settings tab
    The country list, regions, tilts, "buy with" funds and left-out accounts sit on a Targets tab next to Securities. Each fund's region is set on that tab, which lists your held funds. The drawer links to it. The mocks can still move this.
    Screens
  • M8Starting data comes from your 2026 tab
    The first setup fills in the countries and weights from the region table on "Global Index Allocation 2026", with Canada (3.55%) moved to Developed ex-US. The 401(k) and DSP start left out. The "buy with" funds start empty for you to pick. Country weights are public data, but account names stay out of the repo, so excluding those two is a step you take in the UI, not a seed.
    Data
  • M10Sells are shown in dollars, with the funds they'd come from
    With "Allow sells" on, an overweight region shows a sell amount in dollars, not shares. The fund the region buys with may not be the one you hold there (VIU in Questrade, say), so a share count could be wrong. Under the row it lists the region's holdings by account, so you decide what to sell. Sells are rare for you, so this keeps that mode simple.
    Math
  • M11Accounts with only a typed-in balance are left out
    An account where you enter a balance by hand has no funds, so it can't be split into regions. It's listed under "Left out" as a whole account. To count it, enter its holdings instead (the holdings editor already supports this). Today that's likely only the DSP, which you're leaving out anyway.
    Counted
  • M12A "buy with" fund doesn't have to be held yet
    The picker offers any market-priced security, and you can add one by ticker as you do today. Daily price updates also cover every "buy with" fund, so a fund you haven't bought yet still gets current prices.
    Data
  • M9It doesn't record anything
    The drawer is a calculator. It doesn't save the plan or the orders you place. Your next sync brings in the buys. The sheet's "previous market value" column goes, since the app already shows change over time.

Agreed with you

From the brainstorm on 2026-11-14 and 15.

  • A1This first, per-purchase tracking later
    The "invest this amount" tool is the core. It only needs current holdings per fund. Per-purchase tracking (issue #30) comes later and enables per-buy gains, sold-share gains, short vs long term, and the realized-gains report.
    Scope
  • A2Market-size weights by region
    Targets come from each country's share of the global stock market (what your sheet calls "Global Market Distribution"). They are not GDP-based.
    Targets
  • A3Canada isn't tracked on its own any more
    You tracked it while living there. Now it's simply part of Developed ex-US.
    Targets
  • A4An optional US tilt
    You may want to weight the US, your home country, above its market size. It's a setting, and 0 means no tilt.
    Targets
  • A5All accounts by default, with some left out
    Gaps are measured across all investment accounts together. You can leave accounts out; the 401(k) and DSP will probably be left out.
    Counted
  • A6Buys only, unless you turn on sells
    You mostly buy and drift back toward the target over time. A switch, off by default, shows a full rebalance with sells and buys.
    Math
  • A7No account picker
    You type an amount in your base currency (USD). Each region has one "buy with" fund, and the tool suggests dollars and shares of it. Today that's Fidelity's funds. If you invest from Questrade, you change the "buy with" funds.
    Screens
  • A8Countries add up to regions (approach A)
    You keep a country weight list. Region targets are worked out from it, and the tilt is applied on top. The yearly update stays the same as now, and fetching weights automatically later would plug straight in.
    Approaches

Scope

Goals

  • Type an amount, get a buy list in dollars and shares
  • Measured against all counted accounts, with live values and prices
  • Targets you can edit yearly, with an optional US tilt
  • The sheet's calculation bugs gone

Non-goals

  • Per-purchase lots and tax lots (#30)
  • Fetching country weights automatically (later)
  • Placing orders, or recording a plan
  • Splitting a multi-region fund (M2)
  • Stocks vs bonds targets

Anti-goals

  • Suggesting a sell when sells are off
  • A buy list that hides what it left out
  • Numbers that disagree with the Investments page totals
  • Account names or balances committed to the public repo

1 · Targets

  • Country list: each country has a name, a weight (%) and a region. The starting list (M8):
    • US: USA 55.9
    • Developed ex-US: Japan 7.4, UK 4.1, France 2.9, Switzerland 2.6, Germany 2.6, Australia 2.1, Other developed 2.77, Canada 3.55
    • Emerging: China 5.4, Taiwan 1.7, Other emerging 8.32 (South Korea is inside it, as in the sheet)
    These are the numbers from the sheet's region table, carried over as they are. They add up to 99.24%, so the scaling note shows (M6). The sheet's "Global Market Distribution" list says 2.4 for Canada, but the region table used 3.55; this keeps 3.55, which is what your past plans were based on.
  • Region target = the sum of its countries' weights × (1 + tilt), then all regions are scaled to add to 100% (M4, M6).
  • A region with no countries has a 0% target. A region with a target but no "buy with" fund shows "Pick a fund to buy" instead of a share count, and its dollars are still shown.

2 · What gets counted

  • Holdings come from CurrentHoldingsReader, the same source as the Investments page, valued in base currency with the existing RateBook. This keeps both pages in agreement (anti-goal).
  • An account counts unless it's left out on the Targets tab (A5). Only investment accounts (trackingMode: 'investment') are listed.
  • A holding counts toward a region when its security has that region (M2). A fund held in several accounts adds up across them. All other holdings go into the "Left out" list with their values (M3).
  • An account with only a typed-in balance (the reader's per-account placeholder holding) is left out as a whole account and listed under "Left out" (M11). It doesn't appear on the Funds card.
  • Region current value = the sum of its counted holdings. The counted total is the sum across regions.

3 · The calculation

A pure function: inputs are region current values, region targets and the amount, plus a sells flag. Output is a dollar amount per region. Turning dollars into shares is a separate step.

  • After-deposit total T = counted total + amount. Gap per region g = target% × T − current. The gaps always add up to the amount.
  • Sells on: each region's trade is its gap (negative = sell). Afterwards every region is exactly on target. Buys get shares as below. Sells show dollars only, plus the region's holdings by account to choose from (M10).
  • Sells off (default): let G = the sum of the positive gaps.
    • If amount ≥ G: every region with a positive gap gets that gap, and the remaining amount − G is split by target %.
    • Otherwise each positively gapped region gets amount × g / G. Overweight regions get 0.
    • No trade is ever negative. The buys always add up to the amount.
  • Shares: dollars are converted to the fund's currency with RateBook and divided by its latest stored price. Shares are shown to 3 decimals, since Fidelity buys fractional shares. The price's date is shown, flagged if it's more than 3 days old.
  • Also shown per region: current %, target %, and % after the buys, so you can see how close the deposit gets you.
Worked example (sells off)Counted total $8,000: US $4,700, Developed $2,000, Emerging $1,300. Targets 60 / 25 / 15. Amount $1,000, so T = $9,000. Gaps: US 5,400 − 4,700 = +700, Developed 2,250 − 2,000 = +250, Emerging 1,350 − 1,300 = +50. G = 1,000 = amount, so the buys are $700 / $250 / $50, and every region lands on target.

4 · Screens

Described in words here. High-fidelity HTML mocks, with both states of the drawer, come next for your approval before the plan.

  • Investments page: an Invest button in the header opens a right-side drawer that pushes the page (your drawer preference).
  • Drawer:
    • An amount field (base currency) and an "Allow sells" switch, off.
    • A table, one row per region: buy-with fund, current %, target %, buy $ (or sell $), shares at price.
    • A total line.
    • "Left out": a collapsed line with a total that expands to the holdings and left-out accounts.
    • Notes: weights scaled (M6), stale prices, missing "buy with" funds.
    • A "Targets →" link to Settings.
    It recalculates as you type. With no amount yet, it shows only current vs target.
  • Settings → Targets tab (M7), with four cards:
    • Regions: name, tilt %, buy-with fund (picked from your securities), and the resulting target %.
    • Countries: an editable list of name, weight and region, with the total shown.
    • Funds: each held security with a region picker (or "Not counted").
    • Accounts: your investment accounts with an "include" tick box.
  • Empty state: before any targets exist, the drawer says "Set your targets first" and links to the tab.

5 · Data

  • New TargetRegion (id, name, sortOrder, tiltPct Float @default(0), buySecurityId String? → Security, SetNull).
  • The price refresh (src/lib/prices/refresh.ts) also covers every TargetRegion.buySecurityId, so an unheld "buy with" fund keeps current prices (M12).
  • New TargetCountry (id, name, weightPct Float, regionId → TargetRegion, Cascade).
  • Security.targetRegionId String? → TargetRegion, SetNull.
  • Account.inTargets Boolean @default(true).
  • Apply with prisma db push, not migrate dev.
  • The cloud-sync export/import carries the two new tables and two new fields. The tables go after Security, which they reference.
  • Default regions and countries (M8) are created the first time the Targets tab or the drawer loads with no regions, not by a migration. They're public numbers, so they can live in code.
  • Routes under /api/investments/targets (read all, update regions, countries, fund regions and account inclusion) and /api/investments/targets/plan?amount=&sells=, with Zod validation as usual. The plan route returns regions, left-out items and notes, and the drawer renders that.
  • A TargetPlanner service built in services.ts, with holdings, rates and prices injected. The calculation in section 3 is a pure function it calls, unit-tested on its own.
