Why

Travel is a large part of your life, and today trips are tracked with tags. Of the 9 tags, 7 are trips ("2026 - Whistler", …). That has three problems:

  1. Tagging is one transaction at a time. Flights and lodging are booked 2 weeks to 4 months early, and friends' settle-ups and refunds arrive up to 3 weeks after, so finding a trip's transactions means hunting.
  2. The tag list grows with every trip, crowding out the tags that aren't trips.
  3. Analysis goes through the Finance Analyst. You ask it things like "compare the costs of my trips this year, with a category breakdown of each". That should be built in.

You also keep a yearly travel budget as a Goal ("2026 Travel Budget", $10,000, which counts the Travel category group). It belongs with the trips.

  • Inside a trip's dates, about a third of the transactions are not trip spending: salary, gym, subscriptions, groceries at home. Dates alone are not enough.
  • Paying friends (e.g. Adrian Fekete "Whis", −$1,280) is categorized as Lodging or Food. The app cannot tell that money went to a friend.

Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals

  • A trip is a first-class thing: name, start and end dates, optional note. It replaces trip tags.
  • Adding transactions to a trip is a review, not a hunt. The app suggests candidates with fixed, explainable rules, and you tick them. Nothing is added automatically.
  • A Travel page: the year's trips, cost per day, totals, where the money went, and the travel budget, with a year-over-year comparison.
  • A trip page: totals, per day, booked ahead, money back, the category breakdown and every transaction, each one editable through the shared transaction drawer.
  • The travel budget is owned by Travel: created, changed and prompted for there.

Non-goals

  • Side-by-side trip comparison ("not too much comparison side by side"). One yearly list is enough.
  • Automatically assigning transactions to trips: no AI, no auto-add.
  • Detecting money paid to friends.
  • Moving Analytics from its modal to the shared transaction drawer. It's a follow-up on the same principle.
  • Other time ranges in Analytics, and spend in one category across many periods. Those are separate projects.

Anti-goals

  • A read-only transaction list. If you see a wrong row, you must be able to fix it right there.
  • A new colour palette. Use only the existing design tokens (src/app/globals.css, light and .dark).
  • Nagging. The budget prompt must be easy to put off.

Constraints

  • One transaction editor. Every transaction row in Travel opens the existing TransactionDrawer (src/components/transactions/transaction-drawer.tsx), fully editable, with ‹ › stepping through the list being shown. No bespoke editor and no modal.
  • Push drawers over modals (the existing Drawer, DRAWER_PUSH_CLASS).
  • Design tokens only. Everything works in light and dark.
  • Schema changes go through prisma db push, not migrate dev, which fails with P3006 on this repo.
  • Cloud sync lists each table explicitly (src/lib/cloud-sync/sync.ts, types.ts). Every new table and column must be added to export, import and the Zod payload schema, or it is silently lost on another device.
  • Vendor-neutral naming: no bank or aggregator names in domain code.
  • Amounts: negative = spend, positive = money in. Dates are stored in UTC.
  • Currency my call: every Travel figure (trip totals, per day, category bars, the budget) converts to the base currency the way Analytics does (src/lib/analytics.ts, convertAmount). 27 of the 227 trip transactions are on CAD accounts.
  • Net, not gross my call: every Travel figure, the budget included, is spend net of money in. A $72 payback lowers the trip total and the budget's spent figure by $72. Tag goals today add absolute values instead.
  • PR against main; no stacked PRs.
Naming. The page and nav item are Travel (/travel), placed after Analytics in the side nav. The individual items are trips ("+ New trip").

Data model

  • Trip: id, name, startDate, endDate (inclusive calendar days), note (optional), createdAt.
  • Transaction → trip: each transaction is on at most one trip, via a nullable trip reference on Transaction. Deleting a trip clears it from its transactions and keeps the transactions.
  • Dismissed suggestions: the (trip, transaction) pairs you left unticked in a review, so they aren't suggested again for that trip.
  • Split transactions: the parts are what get assigned and counted, never the split parent. Goals use the same rule (isSplitParent: false).

Converting the existing trip tags

This runs once.

  1. A tag is a trip when its name matches ^\d{4} - my call. That catches exactly the 7 trip tags: Austria/Italy, Bayfield, California, Costa Rica, Miami, Mt Tremblant and Whistler. Each becomes a trip, with the "2026 - " prefix dropped, and its transactions are assigned to it.
  2. A trip's dates come from its own Travel-category spending, excluding Flights and Lodging because those are booked early. If it has none, they come from all of its transactions. The trip page shows "Check these dates" until you confirm or edit them.
  3. Safety my call: the conversion copies dev.db to a timestamped backup first, prints a dry-run summary (trips, counts, guessed dates) before it writes, and is idempotent.
  4. The converted tags are removed from their transactions and deleted. Green Card and Tax Deductable are untouched. No goal may be left pointing at a deleted tag.

Suggestions

Suggestions are step 2 of creating a trip, and what "Review suggestions" opens. Candidates are transactions not on any trip, not transfers between your own accounts, not split parents, and not dismissed for this trip. They come in this order:

GroupRulePre-ticked
Booked beforeFlights or Lodging in the 4 months before the start dateYes
DuringTravel category group, dated start–endYes
Other spending duringAny other spending dated start–end, except income and recurring transactions such as subscriptionsNo
AfterThe 3 weeks after the end date: money coming in (except income categories and recurring), plus Travel-category spending such as late rental-car bills, or Anthony "Bills" −$165, which is filed under Drinks and FoodYes
  • Each row shows the merchant, date, category, amount and why it's suggested (e.g. "46 days before"). The footer button is Add N transactions, where N is the number ticked.
  • Rows left unticked are recorded as dismissed for that trip. You can still add one later from its transaction drawer.
  • "Review suggestions · N" on the trip page opens the same list with only the current candidates. N is the number not yet added or dismissed, and the button is hidden at 0.
  • Nothing is ever added without a tick.
  • Editing trip dates my call: dismissals stay dismissed and assigned transactions stay assigned. The trip page groups each transaction against the current dates.

Travel page · /travel · mock A

  • Header: a "Travel" caption, the year as the title, a year chip (‹ 2026 ▾ ›) that works like the Analytics month picker but for years, and + New trip.
  • Three cards:
    • Travel budget: spent, target, a bar that turns red when over, and "$X over" or "$X left". With no budget for the year, it offers to set one.
    • Trips: the count of trips starting that year and their total days.
    • vs last year: this year's budget-spent figure against last year's, or "—" with a note when there's no previous year.
  • Trip list, biggest total first. Columns: Trip · Dates · Where it went · Per day · Total.
    • Where it went is a split bar of the trip's categories in one hue at decreasing strength: the darkest shade is that trip's biggest category. A legend names the top three with percentages, and hovering a segment shows its category and amount.
    • Per day = total ÷ days (inclusive). Clicking a row opens the trip page.
  • "Not on a trip" row below the trips: Travel-category spending that year on no trip, with total and count. It opens a list whose rows open the transaction drawer.

Trip page · /travel/[id] · mock C

  • Header: a "Travel · 2026" caption, the name, dates and day count, Edit trip, and Review suggestions · N when N > 0.
  • Four cards:
    • Total: net of money in, with the transaction count.
    • Per day: with that year's average per day across trips.
    • Booked ahead: spend dated before the start date.
    • Money back: incoming money on the trip, with the count and "already taken off the total".
  • By category: bars in the same tonal scale, biggest first.
  • Transactions: the Transactions table's columns (Date, Merchant, Category, Amount), grouped into Before / During / After. Clicking a row pushes in the shared transaction drawer. The open row is highlighted, and ‹ › step through this trip's transactions.

Trip drawer · mock B

One push drawer for New trip and Edit trip.

  1. Details: Name, Leave, Return and an optional Note, with Cancel / Next: find transactions. A trip can be created before you go, and suggestions then appear as transactions arrive.
  2. Add transactions: the suggestions list.

Edit trip opens step 1 filled in, with Delete trip at the bottom. It uses the same inline confirm as Delete transaction, and deleting keeps the transactions.

Transaction drawer change

DetailsSection gains a Trip field: a select of trips, most recent first, plus "None". It saves immediately, like category and tags. That's how a transaction joins or leaves a trip from anywhere, including the Transactions page and the review queue.

Travel budget

Travel owns it. It is still stored as a Goal, so progress and pace come from one place (src/lib/goals.ts), through a new tracking method.

Which year spending belongs to: by trip decided
A trip belongs to the year it starts in, and all its transactions count there, whatever their dates. That includes flights booked the December before and settle-ups in the next January. Travel-category spending on no trip counts in the year it happened. So the page adds up: budget spent = that year's trip totals + "Not on a trip". A booking made before its trip exists counts in its own year under "Not on a trip", and moves to the trip's year once added. Only the travel budget works this way. Analytics, the other goals and Reports keep counting by transaction date.
  • What it counts: every transaction on a trip starting in that year, plus every Travel-category transaction on no trip dated in that year, each counted once. Lazy small trips still count through their category, and trip spend outside the Travel categories (green fees, a spa) counts through the trip.
  • Where it's managed: created and edited only from the Travel page's budget card, as one budget per calendar year. You set the amount only; the dates are fixed to the year. The Goals page still lists it, marked "Managed in Travel" with a link, and it can't be edited there.
  • The existing "2026 Travel Budget" switches to the new tracking method during the conversion.
  • Yearly prompt: when the current year has no travel budget, a slim app-wide banner says "Set your <year> travel budget" and links to Travel. Not now hides it for 14 days my call. It disappears once a budget exists.

Finance Analyst

queryTransactions filters by tag today. It must also filter by trip, and the analyst needs a way to list trips, so trip questions keep working once trip tags are gone.

Left to the builder

  • API shape, file layout, component split, and whether the trip list and the trip page share loaders.
  • How the conversion runs, as a script or on first visit to Travel. It must be idempotent and must go through sync.
  • Whether the year picker is extracted from Analytics or the pattern is copied. DRY is preferred.
  • How the shades are generated from --accent. The mock uses color-mix.
  • Empty states: no trips in a year, a trip with no transactions.

Testing

  • Unit:
    • each suggestion group's window and categories, and the exclusions: already on a trip, dismissed, transfers, split parents;
    • per-day and total maths, Money back, booked ahead;
    • the budget's counting rule, by trip year, with no double counting;
    • the tag conversion and its date guess.
  • Component:
    • the trip drawer's steps, Add N, and that unticked rows are dismissed;
    • the review button's count, and that it's hidden at 0;
    • a trip page row opens the shared transaction drawer;
    • the Trip field saves immediately;
    • the Goals page shows the budget as managed in Travel.
  • Integration: a sync round-trip includes trips, assignments and dismissals.
